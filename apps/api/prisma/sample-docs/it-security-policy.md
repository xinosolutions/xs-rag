# Acme Corp Information Technology Security and Data Protection Policy

In an interconnected digital economy characterized by sophisticated cybersecurity threats and evolving regulatory standards, safeguarding informational assets, proprietary intellectual property, customer data, and computing infrastructure is paramount to Acme Corp's operational resilience and commercial reputation. The Acme Corp Information Technology Security and Data Protection Policy defines the security standards, user responsibilities, access protocols, and operational guidelines necessary to protect company digital assets from unauthorized access, breach, destruction, or compromise.

Information security is not solely the responsibility of the IT Department or security engineers; it is an active, daily responsibility shared by every individual who accesses Acme Corp networks, cloud systems, email platforms, databases, or computing devices. Maintaining strong cybersecurity hygiene protects our company, our colleagues, and our trusted enterprise clients.

## Information Security Mandate and Scope

This policy applies comprehensively to all Acme Corp personnel, including full-time and part-time employees, executive officers, temporary contractors, vendor partners, and external consultants who utilize company hardware, software applications, electronic communication channels, or cloud environments. It encompasses all corporate computing hardware, remote storage systems, networks, data repositories, and third-party software-as-a-service (SaaS) applications contracted by Acme Corp.

All technological resources provided by Acme Corp are company property and are intended primarily for legitimate business purposes. Acme Corp implements automated monitoring, intrusion detection, endpoint telemetry, and audit logging across all enterprise systems to protect organizational assets and maintain regulatory compliance. Employees should have no expectation of personal privacy regarding communications, files, or network traffic transmitted over or stored upon company systems.

## Password Complexity and Expiration Standards

User authentication credentials serve as the first line of defense protecting enterprise systems and proprietary databases against unauthorized infiltration. All user accounts must utilize robust, complex credentials that resist automated brute-force attacks and credential stuffing tactics. Under Acme Corp access security baselines, Passwords have at least 14 characters and expire every 90 days across all corporate systems, single-sign-on (SSO) directories, and database administrative interfaces.

Passwords must incorporate a diverse mixture of character types, including uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numerical digits, and special symbols (e.g., !, @, #, $, %, ^, &, *). To prevent credential compromise, users must never reuse previous passwords, share credentials with co-workers or supervisors, write passwords down on physical notes, or store passwords in unencrypted plain-text documents. All employees are provisioned with an approved enterprise password manager to generate, securely store, and autofill complex credentials.

## Multi-Factor Authentication Requirements

Given that password credentials alone can be vulnerable to sophisticated phishing and social engineering exploits, Acme Corp enforces strict secondary verification mechanisms across all corporate access gateways. Under company policy, multi-factor authentication is mandatory for all employee accounts, administrative consoles, cloud platforms, corporate VPN connections, and external SaaS services containing business data.

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) must be configured using an enterprise-approved authenticator application (such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, or an authorized hardware security key) that produces time-based one-time passcodes (TOTP) or biometric push notifications. SMS text-message-based authentication is explicitly disbarred as a primary authentication mechanism due to established vulnerabilities related to SIM swapping and cellular interception. Any employee who misplaces their registered MFA hardware device or experiences repeated unauthorized push prompts must alert the IT Security Operations Center immediately.

## Device Security and Disk Encryption

All endpoint devices used to conduct company business—whether corporately provisioned or approved through secure mobile device management programs—must maintain rigorous endpoint security baselines. Because lost, stolen, or misplaced devices pose severe data exposure risks, laptops must use disk encryption using centralized enterprise-managed full-disk encryption software (such as BitLocker or FileVault) with secure cryptographic keys stored in the company key management escrow system.

Employees must never disable, modify, or circumvent endpoint protection controls, host firewalls, mobile device management agents, or automated operating system security patches installed by IT Administration. When stepping away from a workstation—even for a brief duration—users must manually lock their computer screen or activate password-protected screen savers. In the event that an Acme Corp laptop or mobile device is lost, misplaced, or stolen, the employee must report the incident immediately so that remote data wipe protocols can be executed.

## Network Access and Safe Data Handling

Access to Acme Corp internal networks, development clusters, and cloud production environments is governed by the principle of least privilege: users are granted only the minimum level of access required to execute their specific job duties. Privileged administrative credentials must remain isolated from daily web browsing and email activities.

When connecting to corporate resources from home or remote locations, employees must connect via the corporate Virtual Private Network (VPN). Connecting company laptops directly to public or unencrypted Wi-Fi networks (such as those in airports, hotels, or cafes) without an active VPN tunnel is strictly prohibited. Sensitive data, including confidential customer information, source code, and intellectual property, must be stored exclusively on authorized corporate cloud drives and never copied onto unapproved personal storage devices or personal consumer cloud accounts.

## Incident Reporting and Compliance Monitoring

Rapid detection and containment of security anomalies is critical to mitigating the impact of potential security breaches. Employees must remain vigilant against phishing emails, suspicious links, unexpected software installation prompts, or anomalous system behaviors. Any suspected security incident—such as receiving a targeted phishing message, clicking a suspect link, or detecting unusual account activity—must be reported within one hour to security@acme.example.

The IT Security team conducts regular vulnerability scans, automated compliance checks, and simulated phishing assessments to evaluate organizational resilience. Deliberate circumvention of security controls, unauthorized installation of unapproved software, or failure to adhere to data protection mandates will result in administrative revocation of system access and potential disciplinary review.
